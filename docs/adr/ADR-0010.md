# ADR-0010: Adopt "5 external crates maximum" dependency policy

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-08-01
- **Decision Maker**: VUMA compiler team

## Context

A-4 audited the full VUMA dependency manifest
(`docs/research/A-4-pipeline-runtime-tests-deps.md` §"Dependency audit").
The current state, verified by reading `Cargo.toml:57-75`,
`src/ive/Cargo.toml:13-22`, and `Cargo.lock:5-43`, is **8 external
crates total** — 3 declared + 5 transitive:

| Crate           | Declared at                       | Transitive of | Role                                     |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------------------------------|
| `bitflags`      | `Cargo.toml:65`                   | —             | bitset macros for IR/capability flags    |
| `cc`            | `Cargo.toml:75` (build-dep)       | —             | UNUSED (Lean FFI bridge removed; `Cargo.toml:67-75` documents this) |
| `z3`            | `src/ive/Cargo.toml:22`           | —             | SMT solver; the "V" in VUMA              |
| `find-msvc-tools` | (Cargo.lock:21-25)              | `cc`          | MSVC toolchain discovery                 |
| `shlex`         | (Cargo.lock:39-43)                | `cc`          | shell-style tokenizer (used by `cc`)     |
| `z3-sys`        | (Cargo.lock:133-140)              | `z3`          | FFI bindings to libz3                    |
| `log`           | (Cargo.lock:27-31)                | `z3`          | logging facade (used by `z3` crate)      |
| `pkg-config`    | (Cargo.lock:33-37)                | `z3-sys`      | libz3 system-library discovery           |

ADR-0005 (already accepted) deletes `cc`, `find-msvc-tools`, and `shlex`
because the Lean FFI bridge they were retained to support has been
removed (`Cargo.toml:67-75` comment, `build.rs` file-level doc, and
`src/ive/src/verification.rs` "Lean FFI bridge removed" comment all
confirm this). After ADR-0005 lands, the count drops to **5 external
crates**:

| Crate           | Declared at                       | Transitive of | Role                                     |
|-----------------|-----------------------------------|---------------|------------------------------------------|
| `bitflags`      | `Cargo.toml:65`                   | —             | bitset macros                            |
| `z3`            | `src/ive/Cargo.toml:22`           | —             | SMT solver                               |
| `z3-sys`        | (Cargo.lock)                      | `z3`          | FFI bindings                             |
| `log`           | (Cargo.lock)                      | `z3`          | logging facade                           |
| `pkg-config`    | (Cargo.lock)                      | `z3-sys`      | libz3 discovery                          |

This is exceptionally lean for a compiler of VUMA's scope (8 internal
crates per `Cargo.toml:13-23`: `vuma-scg`, `vuma-ive`, `vuma`,
`vuma-bd`, `vuma-parser`, `vuma-codegen`, `vuma-tests`, `vuma-package`;
19 backends; Z3-based IVE; Lean proofs; full gold-standard test
suite). For comparison: a typical Rust compiler project of this size
carries 30–80 external crates. Wave 43 of the cleanup already removed
`serde`, `serde_json`, and `toml` (per A-4's audit), and the workspace
hand-rolls its own `toml_lite` parser (`src/package/src/toml_lite.rs`)
and its own JSON serializer (`src/scg/src/structured_output.rs`,
`src/json_value.rs`) rather than re-pulling those crates. The user's
"small dependencies" mandate is strongly honored.

The risk is policy drift. Future feature work — the GPU stack (V-GPU
in `docs/vuma-side-problem-catalog.md:45`), font parsing, text shaping
(V-26 in `docs/vuma-side-problem-catalog.md:197-222`), TrueType hinting,
variable-font axes — will pressure the policy. Without a hard cap and
an ADR gate, the natural tendency is to add `serde` for one feature,
`bytemuck` for another, `rusttype` for a third, and end up at 15+
crates within a release cycle. The 8-crates → 5-crates reduction
achieved by ADR-0005 is the right moment to lock in a cap, because
the manifest is at its cleanest state in the project's history.

There is currently no documented dependency policy. `docs/contributing.md`
exists (read in full) but has no section on dependencies — its table of
contents (`docs/contributing.md:26-37`) covers Getting Started, Code
Style, PMT-Only Test Policy, Adding a New Backend, Adding a New Test,
VWK Kernel, VUMA Code Patterns, Pull Request Process, and Commit
Messages. Nothing governs when a new external crate may be added.

## Decision

Adopt a hard cap of **5 external crates** for the VUMA compiler
crates, with an ADR gate for any addition.

1. **Hard cap.** The 5 compiler crates — `vuma` (root, `Cargo.toml:1`),
   `vuma-parser` (`src/parser/Cargo.toml`), `vuma-scg`
   (`src/scg/Cargo.toml`), `vuma-codegen` (`src/codegen/Cargo.toml`),
   and `vuma-ive` (`src/ive/Cargo.toml`) — may collectively depend on
   at most 5 external crates (declared + transitive). The current
   post-ADR-0005 set (`bitflags`, `z3`, `z3-sys`, `log`,
   `pkg-config`) sits exactly at the cap. Any PR that increases the
   count above 5 must be rejected by review unless accompanied by a
   new ADR (numbered ADR-0011 or later) that justifies the addition.

2. **ADR gate for any new dependency.** A new external crate may be
   added only via a new ADR that explicitly answers: (a) why the
   functionality cannot be hand-written in ≤200 lines of Rust
   (VUMA's own `toml_lite` and `json_value` are the precedent —
   both replaced a deleted external crate); (b) why no existing
   dependency can be repurposed to provide the functionality; (c)
   the crate's maintenance status, license compatibility with MIT
   (VUMA's license per `Cargo.toml:29`), and transitive-dependency
   blast radius; (d) which existing dependency, if any, the new one
   replaces (the cap is 5, so a net-new addition requires either
   staying under the cap or removing an existing dep).

3. **`womb/` stdlib exemption.** The `womb/` directory (e.g.
   `womb/crypto/mac_kdf/hmac.vuma`, `womb/kernel/**/*.vuma`) is
   VUMA *source code*, not Rust crates. It is exempt from this
   policy — it adds no entries to `Cargo.lock`. The exemption is
   noted explicitly because `womb/crypto/mac_kdf/hmac.vuma`
   contains a hand-written RFC-2104 HMAC-SHA-256 implementation
   that V-16 (`docs/vuma-side-problem-catalog.md:244-263`)
   identifies as the path to replacing the FNV-1a × 4 capability
   signature scheme; the policy must not be misread as forbidding
   that migration.

4. **Dev-dependency exemption (with scope).** `[dev-dependencies]`
   in any of the 5 compiler crates are exempt from the 5-cap, but
   must be limited to test-only crates (e.g. `proptest`,
   `quickcheck`, `criterion`). A dev-dependency that ships in the
   release binary is a violation. The current manifest has zero
   `[dev-dependencies]` entries across all 8 internal crates
   (verified by reading every `Cargo.toml`), so this exemption is
   forward-looking, not a grandfather clause.

5. **Documentation.** Add a new "Dependency Policy" section to
   `docs/contributing.md` (after the existing "Code Style" section,
   between `docs/contributing.md:26-37` items 2 and 3, or as a new
   top-level section — placement at PR discretion). The section must
   restate the 5-cap, the ADR gate, the `womb/` exemption, the
   dev-dependency exemption, and link back to this ADR-0010 and to
   ADR-0005 (the `cc`/`find-msvc-tools`/`shlex` deletion).

## Consequences

### Positive

- The current 5-crates state — the cleanest in VUMA's history —
  is locked in. Future feature work cannot silently re-introduce
  `serde`-class dependencies; each addition must survive an ADR
  review.
- The "small dependencies" mandate (a project-level user
  requirement, per A-4's audit) becomes enforceable in code
  review rather than aspirational.
- Reviewers gain a concrete, numeric acceptance criterion
  ("`cargo tree --workspace` lists ≤ 5 external crates") that
  can be checked in seconds.
- The hand-rolled `toml_lite` and `json_value` parsers
  (`src/package/src/toml_lite.rs`, `src/json_value.rs`) are
  protected from being re-replaced by `toml`/`serde_json` — the
  pattern of "hand-roll rather than pull" is now policy, not
  happenstance.

### Negative

- The cap will bind during GPU-stack work. V-GPU
  (`docs/vuma-side-problem-catalog.md:45`) is 3–6 months of
  greenfield work that will plausibly want `ash` (Vulkan bindings),
  `naga` (SPIR-V/SPIR-V cross-translation), or `bytemuck` (pod
  casts). Each addition will require an ADR justifying why a
  hand-written equivalent is infeasible. For `ash` specifically,
  the Vulkan FFI surface is large enough that hand-writing is
  genuinely impractical — the ADR will likely be granted, but
  the cap will drop to 4 for other features.
- V-26 (`docs/vuma-side-problem-catalog.md:197-222`, "Parser
  lacks const byte arrays") and V-16 (`docs/vuma-side-problem-catalog.md:244-263`,
  "Capability signatures use FNV-1a × 4") both touch areas where a
  dependency would be the easy path (e.g. `sha2` for HMAC-SHA-256).
  The policy forces the hand-roll path (V-16 explicitly identifies
  `womb/crypto/mac_kdf/hmac.vuma` as the source of the
  implementation to plumb in). This is more work but is consistent
  with the project's existing `toml_lite`/`json_value` precedent.
- An ADR-per-addition is overhead. A contributor who needs a
  single small crate (e.g. `byteorder` for endianness helpers)
  must write an ADR rather than just add the line. The overhead
  is intentional — it forces the "can this be hand-rolled in
  ≤200 lines?" question — but it will feel heavy for trivial
  additions.

### Neutral

- The 8 internal crates (`vuma`, `vuma-bd`, `vuma-codegen`,
  `vuma-core`, `vuma-ive`, `vuma-package`, `vuma-parser`,
  `vuma-scg`, `vuma-tests`) are unaffected — they are not
  "external."
- The `rust-toolchain.toml` nightly pin and the `rust-src` /
  `rustfmt` / `clippy` components are unaffected — they are
  toolchain, not crates.
- The Lean toolchain (`proof/lean-toolchain` v4.21.0) and the
  `proof/PMT/**.lean` files are unaffected — they are not Rust
  crates.
- `wasm32_runner.py`, `show_trend.py`, `regen_manifest.py`,
  `run_one_batch.py`, `run_backend_resilient.py`,
  `supervisor.py` (all in `scripts/`) are Python, not Rust —
  they are exempt.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative A: No cap; case-by-case ADRs

Each new dependency gets an ADR, but there is no numeric ceiling.
Rejected because (a) without a cap, "policy drift" is the expected
outcome — each individual ADR is small and reasonable, but the
cumulative trajectory reaches 15+ crates within a release cycle;
(b) the 5-crates state achieved by ADR-0005 is a signal achievement
worth preserving at a numeric level; (c) the cost of the cap is
low when the cap = current state — there is no immediate pressure
to violate it, and the ADR gate only fires when pressure appears.

### Alternative B: Cap at 10 external crates

A more permissive ceiling that would accommodate ~5 future additions
without an ADR per addition. Rejected because (a) 10 is large
enough to re-admit `serde` + `serde_json` + `toml` + `sha2` +
`bytemuck` + `byteorder` + `ash` + `naga` + `rusttype` + `ab_glyph`
without any single addition triggering an ADR — exactly the
"boil the frog" trajectory the policy is meant to prevent; (b) the
cap should bind before the cost of removing a dependency exceeds
the cost of the original addition, and 10 is past that inflection
for a project this size; (c) the user's "small dependencies"
mandate is qualitative — a 10-cap is harder to defend as "small"
than a 5-cap, especially when 5 is the demonstrated steady state.

### Alternative C: Ban all external crates, including `z3`

The strictest possible policy. Rejected because (a) `z3` is the
"V" in VUMA — the entire IVE layer (`src/ive/src/invariant_aggregator.rs`,
`src/ive/src/verification.rs:1925` `discharge_contracts_and_prove_blocks`)
depends on Z3 for contract discharge; hand-rolling an SMT solver
is a multi-year research project, not a dependency choice; (b)
the Lean proof layer (`proof/PMT/Soundness.lean:245-525`,
`pmt_soundness`) and the Rust IVE layer are designed to interoperate
via Z3 — removing Z3 would break the IVE↔Lean parity architecture
documented in `docs/vuma_orchestrator_ive_faithfulness.md`; (c) the
`z3-sys`, `log`, and `pkg-config` transitive deps are unavoidable
consequences of `z3` and cannot be removed without removing `z3`
itself. The 5-cap explicitly accommodates the `z3` family (4 of the
5 slots) plus `bitflags` (1 slot).
